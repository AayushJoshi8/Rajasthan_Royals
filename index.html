Who delivers when it matters?

The 10 batters and 10 bowlers who stay most dependable in high-pressure moments, from 731 IPL and T20I matches in the Rajasthan Royals data pack.

Tap a player to see how they perform under pressure compared with normal play.

Score is the Pressure Reliability Rating (50 is average among eligible players; higher is better). Confidence shows how often the player stays in the top 10 when the data is resampled 400 times: High 60% or more, Medium 40-59%, Lower under 40%. † Pakistan players are ranked on the data but cannot play in the IPL.

  1. Find the pressure momentsA win-probability model scores every ball. The top 15% of balls that can swing the result most, while the match is still open, count as high pressure (26,059 of 174,741 balls).
  2. Judge each ball fairlyEvery ball is compared with what an average player would do in the same situation against the same opposition. Runs and wickets are both counted.
  3. Be careful with small samplesPressure samples are small, so lucky streaks are pulled back toward a player's normal level. This is what keeps one hot innings from making a ranking.
  4. Check consistency and robustnessPlayers are scored on value plus match-to-match consistency, then ranked by how well they hold up across 400 resamples and 11 alternative methods. Players who lost win probability overall under pressure are removed.

What the data says

Pressure balls move win probability about 3 times as much as normal balls, and wickets fall 46% more often. The top of each list is firm; ranks 7 to 10 are close calls and are labelled with lower confidence.

A gap for Rajasthan Royals

No player in the Royals' IPL 2026 squad listing reaches either top 10. Their best pressure bowler ranks 16th and their best pressure batter 35th among eligible players.
